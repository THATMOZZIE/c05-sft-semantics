# 1. Audit verdict on the completed project

## Bottom line

The final Notebook 10 interpretation is scientifically correct:

> Two behaviorally valid model families showed no preregistered qualifying multi-principal scope failure; Gemma 3 4B was assay-invalid; no validated phenotype survived to justify mechanistic work; the branch should terminate.

This is not evidence that language models lack user representations, that persistent memory is safe, or that person-scope failures never occur. It is evidence against the particular frozen in-context scope phenotype on the two models for which the assay was valid.

## What survives

1. **Explicit participant facts can be retrieved under suitable scaffolds.** The earlier 20/20 retrieval control supports generic participant/entity tracking. It does not establish a privileged conversational-user state, spontaneous downstream use, or a dedicated binding mechanism.
2. **Position and prompt structure are load-bearing.** Notebook 06's ownership contrasts split by target position: all eight target-first contrasts were negative and all eight target-second contrasts were positive, with means of approximately `-0.510833` and `+0.240448`. That is much better evidence for position/recency structure than for participant-specific retaliation.
3. **The historical Notebook 07 coded-status effect was scaffold-dependent.** In cleaned 09E, the four relevant mapping-reversal contrasts were approximately `-0.073921`, `+0.000595`, `+0.071552`, and `+0.001572`, while explicit retrieval margins remained approximately `+12.6` to `+17.1`. The preregistered behavioral threshold failed. The semantic and literal-code branches correctly stopped.
4. **Explicit availability and spontaneous behavioral use are different measurements.** The project demonstrated this operationally: information could be retrieved on demand while the formerly reported irrelevant downstream contrast vanished. It did not identify a literal internal gate.
5. **Notebook 09 is inadmissible for its target claim.** Its owner-specific manipulation check failed because candidate priors dominated both the original and preregistered repair. The frozen downstream values therefore cannot adjudicate actual-user amplification.
6. **Notebook 10 supplied a valid negative stopping result.** Qwen2.5-3B and SmolLM3-3B passed prerequisite controls and showed no qualifying preregistered scope phenotype. That is the endpoint, not a prompt-search invitation.

## What died

- Retaliation, resentment, revenge, or persistent negative social-history interpretations.
- A confirmatory reading of 09A: its preregistered expected direction was positive, whereas the observed negative direction was exploratory.
- The 09B ownership-by-position “interaction”: its variables were structurally aliased, so it was not an independently identified causal interaction.
- A semantic-status interpretation of 09C.
- A literal-`K` interpretation of 09C.
- A strong claim that aliases themselves failed to transfer. 09D established failure under a changed alias scaffold, not an isolated alias boundary.
- The Notebook 09 actual-user-versus-Rowan behavioral comparison, because its prerequisite retrieval manipulation failed.
- A validated wrong-owner scope phenotype in Notebook 10.
- Any present justification for probes, steering, activation patching, or circuit work on the terminated owner-scope branch.

## Model-specific Notebook 10 verdict

| Model | Prerequisite validity | Admissible conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Gemma 3 4B IT | Failed; reported `0/16`, with margins spanning roughly `-1.0252` to `+0.7101` | No conclusion about scope cleanliness or failure |
| Qwen2.5-3B Instruct | Passed `16/16`; minimum reported correct margin `13.375`; all 32 canonical cells correct | Healthy self-use, no preregistered stable leakage, pseudo-binding, or collapse |
| SmolLM3-3B | Passed `16/16`; minimum reported correct margin `8.0`; canonical classifier clean | No qualifying preregistered nontrivial failure |

Some descriptive time-domain cross-effects were nonzero on Qwen and SmolLM3. They did not satisfy the frozen cross-domain/structural criteria. They must remain descriptive rather than being promoted into a surviving phenotype.

## Audit issues and their consequences

I found no arithmetic or implementation inconsistency that reverses the final conclusion. I did find documentation/provenance weaknesses:

1. **Notebook 10 lacks a standalone immutable execution plan, raw-results JSON, and submitted-message manifest.** Qwen's displayed raw factorial rows were sufficient for spot verification, but SmolLM3's 32 raw factorial rows were not displayed or saved alongside the notebook. Its `32/32` result is supported by the saved classifier/summary rather than an independently inspectable row table.
2. **Gemma's raw result table is not preserved in the notebook artifact.** The final corrected values were corroborated by the mutable Jupyter state store, which is weaker provenance than a frozen raw JSON.
3. **The qualification description changed from eight time-domain controls to 16 time-plus-weekday controls.** The executed expansion was stricter and occurred before the valid-model results, so it does not favor a positive claim. Calling all 16 “preregistered” without documenting the amendment is nevertheless too strong.
4. **SmolLM3 was named as a reserve before its run, but the explicit replacement of invalid Gemma was documented after the Qwen result and before the Smol run.** “Predefined reserve model” is supported; “fully preregistered replacement rule” is overstated.
5. **Repository history is incomplete.** Many relevant artifacts are untracked, so Git cannot independently prove all timing claims.

These issues lower confidence in provenance quality and the polish of a final artifact. They do not create a hidden positive result, invalidate the two valid clean classifiers, or justify reopening the branch.

## Best honest artifact from the completed work

If no new pivot qualifies, the most defensible artifact is:

> **From apparent social memory to prompt-local pseudo-binding: an adversarial case study of behavioral user-model assays.**

Its contribution would be the falsification sequence: apparent social ownership yielded to position; apparent semantic binding yielded to chat-template adjacency; candidate priors invalidated an actual-user manipulation; and a final preregistered scope assay was clean on two valid families. It must not claim a user-model circuit or a general absence of user modeling.

# 2. Remaining high-value research gaps

## Do strong pivots emerge naturally from the final results?

**No strong positive pivot is compelled by the evidence.** The final results mostly identify how easy it is to manufacture apparent binding and how important it is to separate retrieval from use. Those are methodological lessons, not evidence that a hidden mechanism awaits localization.

There are plausible next questions, but they are either adjacent new projects or bounded attempts to turn a methodological distinction into a testable phenotype. Literature support can motivate them; it cannot convert Notebook 10's null into evidence for them.

## Ranked unresolved gaps

### 1. Personal-context relevance versus generic contextual competition

**Gap:** When a personal fact should be irrelevant to an explicitly specified objective criterion, does it exert more inappropriate causal influence than a structurally matched non-personal fact?

**Evidence that the gap is real:** OP-Bench reports behavioral over-personalization when memory is irrelevant; RPEval reports irrational use of personalized memory; causal-personalization work shows that personalization signals can be intervened on in other tasks. None of those premises establishes that a toy personal-context leakage effect exceeds generic fact competition, or localizes that difference causally.

**Relation to this project:** It follows from the project's strongest operational lesson—availability is not use—but not from a positive project result. It is an adjacent, bounded pivot.

**Worth pursuing?** Yes, once, because a matched generic control makes a positive result nontrivial and a valid null is cheap and terminal.

### 2. Prompt-structural pseudo-binding

**Gap:** Which chat-template or adjacency operations create the appearance that arbitrary context is bound to a repeated label?

**Evidence:** The historical 09C effect vanished when the mapping was moved out of Gemma's system-to-first-user fused region, despite strong retrieval.

**Relation to this project:** This is the most direct natural gap.

**Worth pursuing?** Not as the main next MATS phase. It risks becoming Gemma-specific prompt archaeology, and Racing Thoughts already demonstrates that position/dependency failures can have causal contextualization mechanisms. The existing 09C→09E falsification may be more valuable as a completed negative/confound case study than as an invitation to search for a template circuit.

### 3. Native provenance versus inferred authority role

**Gap:** Under controlled conflict, when do native chat tags versus role-like language determine which source is treated as authoritative?

**Evidence:** UserAssist reports source-role weighting, and Prompt Injection as Role Confusion reports that role-like text can occupy the representational space of a trusted role and predict attack success.

**Relation to this project:** The project learned that chat-template structure matters, but it did not observe a validated provenance-conflict phenotype. This is an adjacent safety project, not a result-derived continuation.

**Worth pursuing?** Potentially by someone seeking direct prompt-injection work, but not in Proposal A's current form and not as the best use of this project's next phase. The closest paper already addresses the central conflict.

## Gaps that should not be pursued

- More social-history prompts intended to recover retaliation.
- More owner-scope semantic domains after two valid clean families.
- A hidden-representation search behind Notebook 10's null.
- Alias transfer repairs after 09C itself failed clean reproduction.
- Probes intended to turn explicit retrievability into a latent-state claim.
- A Gemma rescue run simply because Qwen/Smol were clean.
- Persistent cross-user memory routing without building and studying an actual persistent shared-state system; that is a different systems-level project.

“Unanswered” is not enough. Each of these lacks either a surviving phenotype, an identified gap beyond prior work, or a bounded route to a causal result.

# 3. Independent Proposal C

Full proposal: `RESEARCH_PIVOT_PROPOSAL_CODEX_INDEPENDENT.md`

## Concise description

**Proposal C — Personal Relevance Gate:** Independently vary a valid personal constraint and an objective constraint, then ask a criterion-specific question. Measure whether the personal constraint inappropriately shifts the objective answer, require replication across statement orders and domains, and compare it against an isomorphic generic-entity conflict before any mechanistic work.

The central estimand is not “does the model use relevant context?” It is:

> Does an irrelevant personal fact influence an objective decision more than a matched generic contextual fact does?

## Why I selected it independently

It preserves the project's only robust conceptual lesson—retrievability is not use—without reopening person ownership. It adds the control most likely to prevent self-deception: a personal effect must exceed generic context competition. Correct task selection is explicitly treated as trivial and does not earn mechanism work. The discovery screen is 24 calls on one frozen model and has a hard stop before domain, model, or wording search.

It is not a strong evidence-compelled continuation. It is the highest-information adjacent bet among the candidates.

# 4. Proposal A — Native Provenance vs Inferred Role

## Strongest argument in its favor

It studies the most direct safety problem of the three: untrusted text acquiring the behavioral authority of a trusted native role. If a controlled conflict between native provenance and inferred textual role produced a robust behavioral interaction and a causal intervention transferred that authority error, the result would matter for prompt-injection defenses.

The distinction between interface provenance and inferred speaker role is genuinely sharper than participant identity. UserAssist supports source-role weighting; Role Confusion supports internally represented inferred roles and attack-linked role confusion.

## Biggest weakness

The proposal overstates the novelty of their intersection. [Prompt Injection as Role Confusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12277) already explicitly argues that content labeled as one native role can be internally treated like another because of how it sounds; it uses role probes and relates confusion to attack success. Proposal A may be a controlled replication or refinement, but it is not yet a new research question.

The proposed pilot is also not frozen enough to evaluate. It lists native provenance, value, position, and textual cue, but defers the exact formulas until after inspecting source prompts. It does not specify the messages that make native role and recency independently manipulable. Its discovery-model rule—Qwen or SmolLM3 depending on which reproduces a baseline—creates model-shopping latitude before the core effect.

## Biggest confound

**Native role is entangled with conversational chronology and correction pragmatics.** A later assistant value can be read as a correction of an earlier user value; a later user turn can be read as an update. Merely counterbalancing values does not identify provenance. “User-like” and “assistant-like” textual cues also bundle confidence, imperative style, politeness, and content form.

## Relationship to the completed work

The real bridge is methodological: the project showed that native template structure can dominate an apparent semantic effect. It did **not** observe a valid native-provenance-versus-inferred-role conflict. Proposal A studies authority rather than person binding and is therefore a genuinely different primitive, but also a mostly adjacent project. It is not a rescue of retaliation; it is an attempt to generalize one confound lesson into a safety question.

## Safety and MATS assessment

- **Safety connection:** Direct.
- **Toy-to-deployment distance:** Moderate; synthetic authority conflict is closer to prompt injection than the other toy assays, but it still omits tools, retrieved documents, and an actual attack objective.
- **Positive result that matters:** A conflict effect that survives chronology/correction controls and a causal intervention that changes authority attribution without copying the answer.
- **Result that merely sounds important:** Reproducing a generic user-versus-assistant source preference, or decoding role from activations.
- **What a MATS mentor attacks first:** “Role Confusion already did the conceptual experiment; what exactly is your new causal claim, and how did you separate role from message order?”

**Verdict:** Reject in current form. It would require a new exact question and stronger novelty case, not a larger factorial.

# 5. Proposal B — Selective Context Use

## Strongest argument in its favor

The identical-prefix construction is mechanistically elegant. The factual prefix is the same before a later query selects “time” or “room,” so task-conditioned divergence must arise after the query cue. Qwen2.5-3B is already competent on similar deterministic tasks, and counterfactual residual patching is feasible locally.

## Biggest weakness

The proposed positive phenotype is largely ordinary question answering: use the time fact for a time question and the room fact for a room question. Proposal B says a robust successful selectivity dissociation may itself justify mechanism work, but that risks spending a mechanistic project explaining lexical-semantic retrieval.

The novelty case is also weaker than stated. [OP-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.13722) and [RPEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16621) already study inappropriate versus rational personalized-memory use behaviorally. [Racing Thoughts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02102) already provides causal evidence about contextualization errors and token-dependency timing. Proposal B's exact same-prefix assay may be cleaner than those tasks, but “how later context selects relevant earlier information” is not an empty mechanistic area.

## Biggest confound

**Exact lexical-semantic matching.** A query containing `time` retrieves the sentence containing `time`; a query containing `room` retrieves the sentence containing `room`. Even if a layerwise patch localizes that computation, the result may teach only how a small language model performs basic attribute lookup. Different answer spaces (`09:00`/`17:00` versus `Cedar`/`Maple`) also make raw cross-task comparisons awkward.

## Relationship to the completed work

This is the most direct conceptual extension of `explicitly retrievable != spontaneously operative`. It correctly abandons ownership and social history. What is new is generic task-conditioned context selection; what is inherited is only the measurement distinction and infrastructure. The project's null does not supply a positive behavioral foothold for B.

## Safety and MATS assessment

- **Safety connection:** Moderate and conditional.
- **Toy-to-deployment distance:** Large until the mechanism predicts an actual inappropriate-context failure.
- **Positive result that matters:** A reproducible inappropriate-use or non-lexical selection phenotype with an intervention that selectively induces/removes irrelevant influence.
- **Result that merely sounds important:** Correct time/room selection plus a layer heatmap.
- **What a MATS mentor attacks first:** “Why is this not lexical retrieval, and what did the intervention teach beyond where answer evidence flows?”

**Verdict:** Do not pursue as written. It is a useful generic control architecture, but correct selectivity should not be the project's target phenotype.

# 6. Proposal C — Personal Relevance Gate

## Strongest argument in its favor

It demands an effect that is both a failure and personal-specific: an irrelevant personal fact must shift an objective decision across orders and domains and exceed an isomorphic generic-entity conflict. That makes the behavioral phenotype more informative than ordinary correct selection and gives the first intervention a specificity test.

It also has the strongest fast-kill discipline. Only a frozen 24-call Qwen screen is initially authorized. A clean valid result stops the project before a second model, another domain, generic control, or mechanism work.

## Biggest weakness

There is no internal behavioral foothold for the proposed failure. Notebook 10's two valid families were clean under a related scope assay. OP-Bench and RPEval establish that inappropriate personalization exists in more natural memory settings, but they do not imply that Qwen2.5-3B will show it in this synthetic in-context task.

The discovery prompt also needs one precise revision before freezing: eliminate the pragmatic possibility that the model is rationally optimizing a joint workflow for the user. The queries must request a factual record under **only one named criterion**, not ask what “should” be sent or deposited.

## Biggest confound

**Rational multi-objective accommodation masquerading as inappropriate personalization.** If the user's device accepts PDF and the archive accepts TXT, the model may reasonably treat both as relevant to a file workflow even when the question names the archive. A larger personal effect than a generic converter effect could reflect ordinary cooperative pragmatics, not privileged personal-context routing.

The minimum fix is exact scope wording:

```text
According only to the archive-acceptance rule, which file format does the archive accept?
The device-compatibility rule is outside this question.
Reply with only the file format.
```

and the matched personal query:

```text
According only to the device-compatibility rule, which file format can my device open?
The archive-acceptance rule is outside this question.
Reply with only the file format.
```

This deliberately makes the positive phenotype harder to obtain. That is desirable: an effect surviving an explicit criterion boundary is closer to an inappropriate-use failure.

## Relationship to the completed work

C takes the availability/use distinction but changes the causal variable from *whose fact is this?* to *is this personal fact licensed by the named criterion?* It holds owner identity fixed and does not test social treatment, aliases, or wrong-person scope. The personal-versus-generic comparison is new. This is a disciplined adjacent pivot rather than a continuation supported by a surviving effect.

## Safety and MATS assessment

- **Safety connection:** Moderate-to-direct. The failure class is valid personal context distorting an objective, explicitly scoped decision.
- **Toy-to-deployment distance:** Moderate. It omits persistent memory retrieval and realistic stakes, but directly instantiates the relevance error rather than merely successful personalization.
- **Positive result that matters:** Personal leakage exceeding generic competition across orders/domains, followed by a causal intervention that changes leakage while preserving the objective fact's correct effect.
- **Result that merely sounds important:** Any nonzero personal contrast, especially in one order/domain, or a patch that changes the final answer indiscriminately.
- **What a MATS mentor attacks first:** “Was the personal fact truly irrelevant, and does the mechanism differ from generic conflict resolution?” The revised scope wording and generic control directly address those attacks.

**Verdict:** Pursue only the frozen first screen. Do not commit to the full program until it passes.

# 9. Scientific-interest comparison

Proposal C asks the strongest nontrivial question:

A positive answer would distinguish personalization from generic retrieval. A negative answer cleanly supports the mundane alternative.

# 10. Literature and research-gap comparison

The local `RESEARCH_SOURCE_MATRIX_v2.md` was treated as the project literature/provenance map. A focused primary-source check was used only where it changed the ranking.

## What the closest literature establishes

- **UserAssist:** models behaviorally weight information differently by native source role. It does not isolate referent identity or a causal provenance-arbitration mechanism.
- **Prompt Injection as Role Confusion:** models can internally treat role-like content as belonging to a different/trusted role; role-probe values predict prompt-injection success. It directly crowds Proposal A's claimed intersection. Probe predictiveness is not proof that the probed direction mediates the attack.
- **OP-Bench:** over-personalization, including irrelevant memory use, is widespread in memory-augmented agents; a filtering method reduces it. It does not identify a token-level causal gate or personal-versus-generic specificity.
- **RPEval:** irrelevant personalized memories can distort intent interpretation and a pragmatic-reasoning method improves rational use. It further weakens any claim that behavioral selective personalized-memory use is unexplored.
- **Locating and Controlling Implicit Personalization:** personalization-related internal signals can be causally manipulated in its tasks. It does not establish criterion-specific inappropriate-use routing.
- **Racing Thoughts:** contextualization timing/dependency failures admit causal mechanistic analysis. It weakens Proposal B's broad novelty claim while supporting the feasibility of a narrower timing question.
- **Multi-user/UCC work:** cross-user contamination in persistent/shared-state systems is a real systems concern. It is not evidence for the in-context owner-scope mechanism tested here.

The comparison preserves the project's load-bearing distinctions:

```text
decodability != causal use
causal use != correct binding or scope
source role != referent identity
persona != memory ownership
persistent-state routing != in-context routing
safety-role confusion != person binding
```

## Gap quality

| Proposal | Best description of literature relationship | Gap verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A | Controlled replication/refinement of Role Confusion plus UserAssist | Crowded; exact new claim not specified |
| B | Mechanistic extension of generic contextual selection and personalized-memory relevance work | Real but broad; minimal assay risks being trivial |
| C | Narrow intersection of over-personalization behavior and causal personalization, with generic-context specificity | Plausible adjacent gap; not a demonstrated global novelty claim |

No absence from the local source matrix is treated as evidence of global novelty.

# 11. Mechanistic and MATS comparison

## Proposal A

- Starts from a real safety question, but lacks a frozen minimal experiment.
- Competing hypotheses are named but not yet causally discriminated.
- “Patch a decision-relevant residual state” is too underspecified and could merely transfer authority-correlated answer evidence.
- The first result risks being “role information is present here,” which Role Confusion already approaches.

## Proposal B

- Has the cleanest technical intervention geometry.
- Relevant versus irrelevant counterfactual patches can locate when query-conditioned influence appears.
- The result may still be ordinary attribute lookup. A clean layer/position heatmap is not enough.
- It has the highest probability of producing technically neat but scientifically unsurprising mechanistic output.

## Proposal C

- Mechanistic work is gated on an actual failure, cross-order/domain replication, and personal-over-generic specificity.
- The first causal experiment can test more than location: whether transplanting the personal-fact counterfactual state transfers the inappropriate objective-task shift more strongly than the identical generic-entity intervention while preserving objective self-use.
- A positive result remains interesting even if the mechanism is simple, because the specificity comparison would identify personal context as a distinct source of inappropriate influence.
- If generic and personal interventions are equal, the personal mechanism claim is falsified; the result becomes generic conflict selection.

**MATS winner:** C, conditional on passing its behavioral gate. Without a qualifying behavior, none of the three has earned internals.

# 13. Confound and self-deception risk

## Proposal A

**Likeliest self-deception:** A recency/correction or writing-style effect is relabeled as conflict between trusted provenance and inferred role. The project then searches for stronger “role-like” cues until an interaction appears, despite Role Confusion already supplying the motivating result.

## Proposal B

**Likeliest self-deception:** Basic lexical retrieval is redescribed as a “task relevance gate,” and layerwise patching of answer evidence is presented as a mechanistic explanation of selective context use.

## Proposal C

**Likeliest self-deception:** Cooperative multi-objective reasoning is called inappropriate personalization, or a one-order/domain contrast is promoted after threshold weakening. Explicit single-criterion wording, both-order replication, a second domain, and the generic control are all mandatory before mechanics.

**Highest overall self-deception risk:** A, because both the behavior and the claimed novelty can be manufactured by flexible cue/design choices. B has the highest risk of a technically successful but conceptually trivial mechanism.

## If feasibility/completion dominates

C remains first narrowly because the 24-call screen is terminal and prevents a long failed project. B is the easiest experiment to execute, but has a lower probability of yielding a compelling polished artifact. A is not yet design-ready.

## If intrinsic scientific interest dominates

C remains first. A's topic is important but crowded; B's minimal result is expected; C's positive and negative outcomes distinguish personalization-specific overuse from generic conflict.

The recommendation is therefore robust to reasonable weight changes. Only an overwhelmingly safety-first objective selects A, and that would still require redesign rather than immediate execution.

## 2. Proposal B — Selective Context Use

**Status:** Do not pursue as written.

Best causal geometry and easiest implementation, but the target behavior is too likely to be ordinary lexical-semantic question answering. Useful ideas from B are already incorporated into C's criterion-conditioned counterfactual design; this is not a hybrid recommendation.

## 3. Proposal A — Provenance–Role Conflict

**Status:** Reject in current form.

Strongest direct safety motivation, weakest novelty/identifiability package. The closest paper already studies the key native-role/text-role conflict, and the pilot leaves chronology, cue construction, formulas, and model choice unfrozen.

No proposal earns unconditional commitment. If C fails, do not fall back to B or A. Consolidate the completed negative/confound project or choose a genuinely separate direction after a fresh literature-driven selection.

# 17. First experiment

## Question

Does an explicitly out-of-scope personal constraint causally shift an objective criterion decision on Qwen2.5-3B, after candidate value and statement position are balanced?

## Model and call budget

- Freeze one exact Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct revision and native chat template.
- Eight prerequisite controls.
- Sixteen canonical factorial cells.
- **Total: 24 inference calls.**
- No SmolLM3, generic-entity confirmation, second domain, paraphrase, or mechanism call is authorized by this experiment.

## Exact canonical history

```text
USER:
The following are two independent compatibility records. For each later question,
use only the record explicitly named in that question.

My device can open only PDF.
The archive accepts only TXT.

ASSISTANT:
Understood.
```

Swap `PDF`/`TXT` independently and swap statement order according to condition.

## Exact paired queries

Personal criterion:

```text
USER:
According only to the device-compatibility rule, which file format can my device open?
The archive-acceptance rule is outside this question.
Reply with only the file format.
```

Objective criterion:

```text
USER:
According only to the archive-acceptance rule, which file format does the archive accept?
The device-compatibility rule is outside this question.
Reply with only the file format.
```

This revision removes `should`, `send`, and `deposit`, which otherwise invite rational joint workflow optimization.

## Factors

| Factor | Levels |
|---|---|
| Personal/device constraint `P` | PDF / TXT |
| Objective/archive constraint `G` | PDF / TXT |
| Requested criterion `T` | device / archive |
| Statement order `O` | personal-first / archive-first |

Full `2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 16` factorial. Analyze order blocks separately.

## Eight prerequisite controls

### Four dual-fact direct-retrieval checks

Use four Latin-square conditions so each referent is queried at both positions and both values across the set:

1. `P=PDF` first, `G=TXT` second; ask for `P`.
2. `G=PDF` first, `P=TXT` second; ask for `P`.
3. `P=TXT` first, `G=PDF` second; ask for `G`.
4. `G=TXT` first, `P=PDF` second; ask for `G`.

Each question asks only for the named recorded format. Score the exact correct and incorrect strings.

### Four isolated application checks

Run one-fact histories for:

- device=PDF → device query;
- device=TXT → device query;
- archive=PDF → archive query;
- archive=TXT → archive query.

Every control must satisfy:

```text
M_correct = log P(correct string) - log P(incorrect string) >= 2.0
```

Candidate boundaries and the rendered native template must be audited and frozen before inference.

## Primary metric

```text
M = log P(PDF) - log P(TXT)
```

Within each statement-order block, define:

- `S_P`: effect of changing the device fact on the device query;
- `S_G`: effect of changing the archive fact on the archive query;
- `I_P`: effect of changing the device fact on the archive query;
- `I_G`: effect of changing the archive fact on the device query;
- `lambda_P = abs(I_P) / abs(S_P)`.

Relevant self-effects must be correctly signed, every canonical cell must prefer the criterion-correct answer, and combined-context self-use must retain at least 50% of isolated application strength.

## Competing outcomes

- **Criterion-appropriate use:** `S_P` and `S_G` are healthy; `I_P` is below threshold in either order. Stop the direction.
- **Candidate inappropriate personal use:** `I_P` follows the personal fact with the same sign, satisfies both thresholds in both orders, and objective self-use remains healthy. Only then may the already-frozen generic-entity and second-domain confirmations be run.
- **Generic conflict/structural effect:** cross-effects disagree by order, self-use collapses, or direct correctness fails. Stop; do not pool or reinterpret.
- **Invalid assay:** prerequisite controls fail after implementation correctness is verified. Stop for this direction; do not prompt-search.

No behavioral effect from this 24-call screen alone earns mechanistic work. A positive screen must still pass the prewritten generic-entity and second-domain confirmations in Proposal C.

# 18. Kill rule

The smallest valid result that terminates the winning direction is:

> **Qwen passes all eight controls, but in either statement-order block the personal-to-objective effect fails either `abs(I_P) >= 0.50` or `lambda_P >= 0.20`.**

Also terminate if the sign differs across order blocks, relevant self-use is unhealthy, or any verified prerequisite control fails.

After that result:

- do not change formats, criteria, names, wording, thresholds, or model;
- do not run SmolLM3 as a rescue;
- do not fall back to Proposal A or B;
- do not inspect activations;
- consolidate the existing negative/confound artifact.

If the screen passes, that is only permission to run the two already-frozen behavioral confirmations. Mechanistic work is earned only if the effect reproduces in the second domain and personal leakage exceeds the matched generic-entity leakage by the frozen minimum.
